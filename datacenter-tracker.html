Tracker · Public tech money

Idaho’s data center rush, tracked.

Developers want to put some of the largest electricity customers in the state’s history in Kuna, Pocatello, the desert south of Boise and beyond. This page follows every project on the public record: who is building, how much power it wants, what tax break it can claim, and what local governments have done about it. It is rebuilt from public records every week.

Kuna · Ada County

Meta Kuna Data Center

OperatingLast change Sep 4, 2026

Developer
Meta Platforms (utility customer of record: Brisbie, LLC)
Size
960,000+ sq ft
Investment
$1.2B Meta’s 2026 figure; $800M when announced in 2022
Status
Serving traffic as of September 2026, according to a Meta executive’s post reported by Baxtel. Announced February 2022 with about 100 permanent jobs.
Tax break
BoiseDev describes Meta as eligible for the 63-3622VV sales tax exemption. 2025 Senate committee minutes record conflicting testimony on whether the Tax Commission granted it. No public record shows the amount. Our note.
Power
Special contract with Idaho Power under the name Brisbie, LLC, approved by the PUC May 11, 2023 (case IPC-E-21-42). Meta says it added 645 MW of new renewable supply in Ada County to match its use. Neither Meta nor Idaho Power has published the site’s load in megawatts.
Water
Meta transferred about 2.5 million gallons a day of farm water rights to the City of Kuna and runs the site on a separate system. Expected summer peak use is just under 2 million gallons a day, BoiseDev reported.
Local rules
Inside the Kuna East urban renewal district, created in 2022.

Who pays for the power

Idaho rewrote the rules this year. The bill is still being split.

Two 2026 laws now govern new data centers. One makes any new load of 50 MW or more pay its full cost of service under a contract the Public Utilities Commission must approve. The other bars new data centers from consuming water for cooling unless it comes from a city or water district system. How Idaho Power’s costs are divided between big new customers and everyone else is being argued in an open PUC case right now.

  1. May 11, 2023

    PUC approves Meta’s power contract

    Order 35777 in case IPC-E-21-42 set the terms for Brisbie, LLC: the first 20 MW at standard large-customer rates, everything above at Idaho Power’s marginal cost. source

  2. Mar 31, 2026

    Idaho Power opens the cost case

    Its petition in IPC-E-26-07 asks the PUC to review how costs are assigned to customer classes and what to weigh for new large loads. It proposes no new tariff. source

  3. Apr 2, 2026

    Cooling water law signed

    HB 895 (Idaho Code 42-252): data centers that begin construction after July 1 may not use water consumptively for cooling unless it comes from a city or water district system. source

  4. Apr 10, 2026

    Large-load law signed

    HB 911 (Idaho Code 61-335): from July 1, new loads of 50 MW or more need a PUC-approved contract that passes a no-harm test, pay their full cost and post security against stranded costs. source

  5. May 8, 2026

    Micron’s contract approved

    The PUC approved Micron’s fab power contract with changes and sent remaining cost questions to the open cost case. source

  6. Oct 29, 2026

    Idaho Power: about 4,000 MW in the pipeline

    Executives told investors the large-load pipeline is “right about 4,000 megawatts,” about a quarter of it promising, and that Meta began operating in September. source

  7. Nov 25, 2026

    PUC staff and intervenors file

    First public positions from PUC staff, Micron, irrigators, industrial customers, Boise and clean energy groups. source

  8. Dec 14, 2026

    Public comment deadline

    Last day for written public comments in IPC-E-26-07. source

  9. Jan 19, 2027

    Technical hearing

    At PUC offices in Boise, continuing January 20 and 21 if needed. source

Have your say

Public comments on large-load costs close December 14.

Case IPC-E-26-07 decides the method for splitting Idaho Power’s costs among customer classes, including how new large loads are treated. Anyone can comment through the PUC’s case page or by email to the commission secretary. The technical hearing starts January 19 in Boise.

Open the PUC case

The tax break. Since 2020, Idaho Code 63-3622VV has let data centers that promise $250 million in investment and 30 jobs skip sales tax on servers, equipment and buildings. The state has never published what it costs, and no law requires anyone to report who claims it.

What we don’t know yet

Open questions.

  1. Which companies hold Idaho’s data center sales tax exemption, and what it has cost. The Tax Commission publishes neither.
  2. How many megawatts Meta’s Kuna campus draws today, and how much more its planned buildings would add.
  3. Which project is the “unnamed” data center Idaho Power has added to its preliminary 2027 load forecast.
  4. Whether any contract has been filed under the new 50 MW large-load law, which starts a 270-day PUC review.
  5. Whether DOE has picked a developer for the INL site, and what Boise’s moratorium covers if it passes on November 10.

Notes on the numbers

Megawatt and dollar figures are what developers or public bodies announced. They measure different things (computing capacity, utility load, on-site generation) and should not be added together. Kuna’s figures show the gap: Meta’s 2026 investment figure is $1.2 billion, up from $800 million at announcement, and estimates of its water use differ by outlet.

We leave out small server rooms and colocation sites listed only by commercial directories, and Avista’s 500 MW data center customer, which Avista places in Washington.

Method

How this is built.

We list a project once a developer, utility, regulator or local government has put it on the public record, and keep it after a denial so the history stays visible. Sizes and dollar figures are what the developer or a public body announced, not what has been spent. Every row links to its records and shows the date of its latest change.
