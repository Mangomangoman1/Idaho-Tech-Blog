## What would make this a useful new piece?

1. Read the minutes and agenda; identify what was decided and when. Locate an
   executed agreement or explicit approval record before presenting final terms.
2. Inspect the city's map and a current authoritative service boundary. Explain
   one concrete trip constraint without inventing a resident, testing a booking
   or implying a point outside the boundary is served. Label proposed maps.
3. Reconcile current fare, hours, reservation horizon, telephone/app access and
   accessible-vehicle guidance through public operator/city records. Link readers
   to the current official guide rather than reproduce unresolved instructions.
4. For an outcomes angle, wait for a dated, defined reporting period and public
   data. Distinguish trips from unique riders, planned service from completed
   rides, city contribution from total cost, and posted terms from spending.
   October is not complete on the research date. No numerical benefit claimed.

**Possible synthesis, still analysis:** a booking app can pool requests, while
coverage, hours, funding and physical pickup arrangements determine usable access.
The useful local question is which cross-city trips became practical and how
residents can verify them. An app announcement alone cannot answer it. An article
needs a verified change or outcome beyond the already reported ribbon-cutting.
