Blaine County’s Power Line Surcharge Has No Price Yet, With the Line Due This Year

Wood River Valley customers will pay a 20-year monthly surcharge for burying parts of Idaho Power’s second line into the valley. The 2021 figure is still the one posted, the 2024 update never took effect, and the final number waits on a filing that has not been made.

By: The Idaho StackPublished: November 10, 2026

Based on Idaho Public Utilities Commission filings and Idaho Power’s own pages. No interviews were conducted.

Idaho Power says its second transmission line into the northern Wood River Valley, the 138-kilovolt Wood River to Ketchum line between Hailey and the Ketchum–Sun Valley area, is under construction and “on track to be complete in late 2026,” according to its project page. When it is, Blaine County customers start paying for the parts the county asked to have buried. How much they will pay has not been filed.

What the records show

2021 estimate. In its 2021 petition, Idaho Power put the extra cost of burying 1.4 miles of the new line and about eight miles of existing distribution line along Buttercup Road and Highway 75 at about $9.8 million: $5.7 million for distribution, $4.1 million for transmission. It proposed a flat surcharge of $3.42 a month for homes and small businesses and $14.36 for other customers, for about 20 years. The commission approved the surcharge and the Owl Rock Road route in 2022, and the approved tariff was filed that July.

2024 update. In a May 2024 compliance filing, the company said the distribution work had cost about $20.0 million, against an estimated $11.8 million for keeping it above ground. After subtracting about $2 million it credited to the transmission side, it put the extra distribution cost at $6.2 million. It proposed a distribution-only surcharge of $2.00 and $8.02 from December 2024, with a second, transmission surcharge to follow in 2026.

2025 change of plan. That case was suspended on November 1, 2024. In 2025 the company asked instead to hold the distribution costs as construction work in progress until the whole line is done, then file one surcharge for the project. The commission agreed to the delay in an August 2025 order, after receiving no public comments. The order gives no dollar figures. Commission staff said the financing costs that build up in the meantime, known as AFUDC, would be “allocated to Blaine County” rather than to other customers.

Today. Idaho Power’s project page and its Schedule 96 tariff still show the 2021 rates of $3.42 and $14.36.

What is still unknown

The actual extra cost of the buried transmission segment; how much financing cost has accrued since 2024; when the final-cost filing will come; and what the combined monthly charge will be. Staff’s position, recorded in the 2025 order, is that once the line is operating the company “would need to file an update reflecting the final costs for the entire project.” That filing will open a comment period at the commission, which is when Blaine County customers can weigh in.

Sources and limits

  • Idaho Power Schedule 96, Blaine County undergrounding surcharge

The 2024 figures were the company’s proposal and never took effect. The final surcharge depends on costs the company has not yet filed.

Next filing

Final-cost surcharge

Idaho Power files it with the Idaho Public Utilities Commission once the line is operating. That filing opens public comment.
