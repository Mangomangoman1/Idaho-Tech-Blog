TerraVolt’s Declo Gas Deal Is Sized at About an Eighth of All the Gas Idaho Uses

TerraVolt Holdings has reserved 55,000 MMBtu of natural gas a day for the off-grid data center campus it wants to build outside Declo. That is about 12 percent of all the natural gas Idaho used in 2024, and more than a third of what every gas-fired power plant in the state burned that year. The company’s newest quarterly report shows its cash, raised mostly through a loan from a director’s family trust, fell to about $4 million by Sept. 30, and that it still had not secured the land.

By: The Idaho StackPublished: November 27, 2026

Based on public SEC filings and federal energy data. No interviews were conducted.

The size of the gas

At a typical 1,037 Btu per cubic foot, 55,000 MMBtu a day is about 53 million cubic feet, or about 19.4 billion cubic feet a year. The U.S. Energy Information Administration counts 156.4 billion cubic feet of gas used in Idaho in 2024 by every home, business, factory and power plant. Gas delivered to Idaho’s power plants that year came to 53.0 billion cubic feet.

The reservation matches the plant TerraVolt describes. Running 240 megawatts around the clock takes 5,760 megawatt-hours a day; 55,000 MMBtu over that is about 9,500 Btu per kilowatt-hour, a normal rate for engines or simple-cycle turbines. In other words, the gas is sized to run the whole first phase flat out.

What the new filing shows

TerraVolt’s 10-Q for the quarter ended Sept. 30, filed in November, updates the numbers we reported Nov. 9 from its June report:

  • Cash: $4.03 million, down from $10.7 million at June 30. Another $6 million is restricted, pledged behind a standby letter of credit issued in August for the gas supplier. That letter runs to March 31, 2029.
  • Debt and losses: $17.4 million in liabilities against $13.6 million in assets, and a $1.66 million loss for the quarter. The going-concern warning stays.
  • Land: TerraVolt signed its two option agreements on the Declo farmland in October, after the quarter closed. As of Sept. 30, the filing says, the company “had not secured land to be used for the proposed data center campus.”
  • Gas terms: TerraVolt must give notice of a start date for deliveries by April 30, 2028, and the start can be no later than July 31, 2029. It then gets a three-year delivery period. Before gas flows it owes a letter of credit of up to $50 million.

April 30, 2028 is also the day TerraVolt’s $16 million note to SFO IDF, the trust-owned lender tied to director Sean Fontenot, comes due.

What we don’t know

Who the gas supplier is; what happens after the three-year delivery period, when data centers usually run for decades; whether the Williams Northwest Pipeline can carry 53 million cubic feet a day to Declo without new pipe or compression; and when TerraVolt will apply to the Idaho Department of Environmental Quality for an air permit and to Cassia County for the zoning changes the campus needs.
