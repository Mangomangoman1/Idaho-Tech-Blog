Idaho’s $120 Million Broadband Builds Have Two Months Left

Eighteen federally funded broadband projects are due December 31. The state’s own report says “79% of projects on track” but doesn’t say which ones aren’t.

By: The Idaho StackPublished: November 1, 2026AI-assisted reporting

Researched and written with AI from public documents, then reviewed and approved by the editor. No interviews were conducted. We are sending questions to the Idaho Office of Broadband and will update this note with any response.

Eighteen broadband projects paid for with Idaho’s share of the federal Capital Projects Fund are due to be finished by December 31, 2026. The Idaho Broadband Advisory Board awarded the $120 million in December 2023 to 15 recipients, who added more than $58.8 million of their own, according to the state.

The clearest public look at progress is the state’s annual performance report to the U.S. Treasury, covering July 2025 through June 2026. The Idaho Office of Broadband posted it in July in a file marked Draft V3. Its progress graphic says “79% of projects on track.” Fourteen of 18 would be 77.8%; fifteen would be 83.3%. The report doesn’t say how the figure was calculated or which projects make up the rest.

The projects the report flags

The report does name problems. These are the projects it singles out:

Some work is finished. Lincoln County’s build is complete, with more than 294 locations passed and 185 active connections. The report calls Ziply Fiber’s Aberdeen project complete, with service available to 734 locations. Comcast’s Preston-area build reached about 96% construction completion during the year.

The report says little about Latah County’s $15 million dark fiber project, the largest single award.

What changed after the report closed

In May, Treasury updated its Capital Projects Fund FAQs to let states ask for a six-month extension, to June 30, 2027, for broadband builds. Requests were due July 31, 2026, and only the state, not providers, can file. Treasury says it will approve one only if a project has made material progress, spent money on it before May 31, 2026, and been delayed by circumstances beyond the recipient’s control.

The report period ended June 30, so it doesn’t say whether Idaho asked, or for which projects beyond Gooding County.

One caution on the headline numbers. The report counts more than 30,000 locations “passed or able to subscribe to service” and more than 4,000 active subscribers, but much of the network was still being built, so the two don’t make a take rate. Where builds are finished, it gives one: about 63% in Lincoln County and 35% in MiFiber’s completed areas.

What we’re asking

We are asking the Idaho Office of Broadband which projects are not on track, whether Idaho requested Treasury extensions and for which projects, and how much of the $120 million has been paid out so far. The report gives no spending figure for the broadband projects. We’ll add the answers here.
