Idaho’s Tax Collectors Ask Again for $550,000 a Year for a Vendor’s Scoring System

The State Tax Commission wants $550,000 a year in ongoing money for a cloud system that scores and ranks people and businesses who owe back taxes. Its budget files show lawmakers already added that same amount to its ongoing base in 2023. The request doesn’t say why it is asking again, and it credits the system with $53.7 million without showing the math.

By: The Idaho StackPublished: November 12, 2026

Based on the Tax Commission’s FY2028 budget request and the Legislature’s February 2026 base review. No interviews were conducted.

Idaho’s tax agency has used FAST Enterprises’ GenTax software for years. In April 2023 it signed a statement of work for something separate: FAST Collection Services, which the document describes as “centralized cloud-based analytics, scoring, and prioritization for delinquent accounts,” a FAST-hosted product “installed outside the agency’s GenTax environment.” It covers individual and business debts and adds pre-approved payment plan recommendations through online services. The schedule put rollout in October 2023, with comparison reporting to follow.

What the records show

2023. The Legislative Services Office’s base review of the Tax Commission, presented to the budget committee on February 2, 2026, lists “FAST Collection Services” at $550,000 among the agency’s 2023 ongoing enhancements. Its five-year snapshot also shows a $550,000 ongoing base adjustment. The review lists no later cut to that line.

2028 request. In the agency’s FY2028 request, decision unit 12.01, “FAST Tax Collection Services,” asks for $550,000 in new ongoing General Fund money, “based on FY26 actual expenditure ($550,000).” The justification says “FY26 collections totaled $53.7 million” and that canceling the service “would reduce General Fund revenue by $20-50.4 million annually based on historical loss scenarios.” The request does not lay those scenarios out.

Scale. The agency asks for $63.9 million in all funds, up $7.5 million from its $56.4 million FY2027 appropriation. Separately, it asks $2.78 million one-time to replace servers, switches and laptops.

What is still unknown

Whether the 2023 money left the base, for example in the $1.37 million FY2027 rescission the base review lists without saying what it cut, so that this request restores it, or whether the service is being requested twice. Whether the $53.7 million is every dollar collected on accounts the system scored, or only what it added over what collectors would have brought in anyway. The vendor’s baseline and comparison reports, which the statement of work lists as deliverables, are not in the budget file. Nor is how the system decides whose account is worked first.

Sources and limits

Public records reviewed November 11 and 12, 2026.

  • Idaho State Tax Commission, FY2028 budget request (Division of Financial Management), with FAST statement of work dated April 13, 2023
  • Legislative Services Office, Tax Commission base review, JFAC, February 2, 2026

The $53.7 million and the $20 million to $50.4 million range are the agency’s own figures. A budget request is not an appropriation; lawmakers set the FY2028 budget in the 2027 session.

Next step

JFAC hearing

The budget committee hears the Tax Commission’s request in the 2027 session, starting in January.
