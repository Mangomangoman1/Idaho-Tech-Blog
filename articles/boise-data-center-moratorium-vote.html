Boise Votes Tuesday on a Data Center Moratorium. Here’s What It Would and Wouldn’t Stop.

The City Council takes up an emergency pause on “large data centers” at 6 p.m. Tuesday. No such project has applied inside Boise, a new data center the city approved last year appears to fall under the size line described for the ordinance, and the biggest proposal near Boise sits on federal land the city can’t touch.

By: The Idaho StackPublished: November 9, 2026AI-assisted reporting

Researched and written with AI from public documents, then reviewed and approved by the editor. No interviews were conducted. We will update this note after Tuesday’s vote.

Mayor Lauren McLean announced the proposal on November 6: “in the absence of a state-wide moratorium, I’m pulling the emergency brake to protect Boiseans and our precious natural resources.” The city says it has no applications for what it calls mega data centers.

On Tuesday’s council agenda it is ordinance ORD-45-26, “an emergency ordinance adopting a temporary moratorium prohibiting land use entitlements for large data centers.” Its title includes a waiver of the reading rules, and the agenda says a vote may be taken on every item, so the council could adopt it the same night. If it passes, the city says, it takes effect immediately.

What counts as large

According to an advance draft shared with the Idaho Statesman, the ordinance covers any facility over 100,000 square feet, or one drawing 20 megawatts from any source, for six months. The city attorney’s cover letter says that without rules written for data centers, “the city is not adequately equipped to process, evaluate, and decide upon an application for a large data center.” State law sets the outer limit: under Idaho Code 67-6523, an emergency moratorium can last no more than 182 days and can’t be renewed back to back. Anything longer needs a regular ordinance with full notice and hearings, which is what the pause is meant to buy time to write.

What it wouldn’t stop

Boise approved a new data center last year. ValorC3’s Emerald site on West Emerald Street won city approval for a 38,000-square-foot, 10-megawatt first phase, and a preliminary application would add 31,000 square feet, about 70,000 in all, BoiseDev reported. Both figures sit under the thresholds described for the ordinance.

The largest proposal near the city is out of Boise’s reach. Rhea Data, a subsidiary of Arevia Power, has asked the Bureau of Land Management to use more than 3,000 acres of public land near Blacks Creek Reservoir, south of Boise, for about 4 million square feet of data center buildings, according to the Idaho Statesman’s reporting. Federal land outside city limits is the BLM’s decision.

Boise isn’t first

Mountain Home adopted an emergency moratorium on October 27, Blaine County on October 6 and Bannock County on July 30; Pocatello denied a $2.6 billion campus in September. At the state level, lawmakers this year passed House Bill 911, which requires new loads of 50 megawatts or more to pay their full cost of service under a contract the Public Utilities Commission must approve, and House Bill 895, which limits consumptive water use for cooling some data centers. Neither pauses development. Every local action is mapped, with sources, on our Idaho data center tracker.

Also Tuesday night

An hour after Boise’s meeting starts, Cassia County commissioners attend a community meeting in Declo on TerraVolt’s proposed 240-megawatt gas-powered campus. No action is scheduled there. Read what TerraVolt’s SEC filings show.
