SpaceX Now Puts Up the Most Company Money in Idaho’s Broadband Awards. The State Hasn’t Said What It Is.

After four providers handed back their awards, about $139.7 million in company money still backs Idaho’s federal BEAD broadband awards. The biggest single piece, $32.7 million, is SpaceX’s “applicant match” for Starlink service, and none of the state’s published records say what that match is.

By: The Idaho StackPublished: November 25, 2026

Based on public state records. No interviews were conducted.

What’s left

When federal regulators approved Idaho’s awards on Jan. 26, the state’s award table listed $426.2 million in BEAD money and $201.9 million in matching money from the providers, for 91,281 homes and businesses. The Oct. 1 Broadband Advisory Board packet then took out four providers that withdrew: $164.4 million in BEAD money, $62.2 million in their match and 25,393 locations. That leaves $261.9 million in BEAD money, $139.7 million in provider match and 65,888 locations. Projects from the state’s August re-bid, $30.2 million more in match, are still in federal review and aren’t counted here.

SpaceX covers 36% of the locations still in the program with 14% of the BEAD money, $1,500 a location. Its match is the largest of the 19 providers left, ahead of Direct Communications Rockland at $25.6 million and ETS Management at $20.8 million. Eleven of the 19 put up between 25% and 30% of their project costs. SpaceX put up 48%, almost a dollar of its own for every federal dollar; only three telephone companies, on smaller awards, went higher.

What a match can be

Under Idaho’s July 2026 subgrant agreement, a match can be “cash or in-kind contributions,” has to be committed and documented before the agreement is signed, and can be checked against invoices. The state’s subgrantee FAQ says providers value their own in-kind assets, which the Office of Broadband and federal regulators review for “accuracy and reasonableness,” and that each provider’s match was approved on Jan. 26 and is binding.

The agreement calls SpaceX’s kind of award a subgrant for “capacity on a Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellite network,” with its own addendum and payment schedule. A fiber company’s match buys trenches, cable and labor. For a satellite network already in orbit, it isn’t clear what $32.7 million covers: dishes, installation, network capacity or cash. None of the state records we read break it out, and the board packet doesn’t say whether SpaceX is among the providers behind the 45 projects with signed agreements.

What we don’t know

Whether SpaceX’s match is cash or in-kind, and how it was valued; whether SpaceX has signed its Idaho agreement; and how its payments are scheduled. We’ve asked the Idaho Office of Broadband what the match consists of and how it was valued, and we’ll add its answer here. The state’s earlier answers on the four withdrawals are on our Idaho broadband tracker.
